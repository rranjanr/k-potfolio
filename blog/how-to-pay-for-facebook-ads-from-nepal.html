How to Pay for Facebook Ads from Nepal (2026 Guide)

By Vaishali Sharma, graphic designer and social media marketer in Biratnagar · 6 min read · September 27, 2026

Quick answer: Meta bills Facebook and Instagram ads in foreign currency, and normal Nepali debit cards do not work for it. The two common options are a dollar card (a USD prepaid or dual-currency card issued by a Nepali bank) added to your own ad account, or a local ad agency or service that runs ads for you and takes payment in rupees. For a business that advertises regularly, a dollar card on your own account is usually the better long-term choice.

"How do I pay for boosting from Nepal?" is one of the first questions every small business owner asks, and the answer is not obvious. You press Boost, Facebook asks for a card, and your regular bank card gets declined. Here is how payment for Meta ads works from Nepal, and how to pick the right option.

Why your normal card does not work

Meta charges advertisers in foreign currency. Nepali debit cards issued in rupees are generally not enabled for international online payments, so Facebook declines them. To pay Meta directly, you need a card that is allowed to make payments in US dollars.

Option 1: A dollar card on your own ad account

Several Nepali banks issue USD prepaid cards or dual-currency cards that can be used for international online payments, including Facebook and Google ads. You load the card with dollars through your bank and add it as the payment method in Meta.

How to get one

  1. Ask your bank whether it issues a USD prepaid, virtual dollar or dual-currency card, and what documents it needs (usually your citizenship or passport, and sometimes a PAN for business use).
  2. Load the card with the amount you plan to spend.
  3. In Meta Business Suite or Ads Manager, open Billing and payments and add the card as a payment method.

Limits to check

Dollar cards in Nepal come with an annual spending limit set under Nepal Rastra Bank rules. For individual cards this has long been a few hundred dollars a year, and the rules have been changing, so ask your bank for the current limit before you plan a big campaign. Businesses with larger ad budgets should ask their bank about options for business or IT-related foreign currency payments.

Pros and cons

  • Pro: the ad account, its history and its data stay yours
  • Pro: you pay Meta's actual price, with no reseller margin
  • Con: annual limits and bank paperwork
  • Con: exchange rate and possible bank fees

Option 2: Pay a local agency or ad service in rupees

Many agencies and online services in Nepal run ads for you and accept payment in NPR through bank transfer or digital wallets. They pay Meta with their own cards and bill you in rupees.

  • Pro: no dollar card needed, and you pay in rupees
  • Pro: good for occasional boosts
  • Con: a service charge is usually added on top of the ad cost
  • Con: if ads run on the agency's account, the results and audience data sit with them, not you

If you use this route, ask whether ads will run on your ad account or theirs, and ask for a report showing the actual amount spent with Meta.

Option 3: A card from family abroad

Some people add a relative's foreign card to their ad account and pay the relative back in Nepal. It works, but it is fragile: if the card owner disputes a charge or the card expires, the ad account can be restricted. It is not a setup I recommend for a business that advertises every month.

Which option should you choose?

Your situationBest option
You boost once in a whileA local service paid in rupees
You advertise every monthA dollar card on your own ad account
You spend a lot on adsTalk to your bank about business foreign currency options
You hire a freelancer to manage adsYour own card on your own account, with the freelancer added as a partner

Keep the ad account in your name

Whatever you pay with, the most important thing is that the ad account belongs to your business. When I manage ads for clients, I work through partner access in Meta Business Suite. The client keeps ownership of the page, the ad account and the payment method, and can see every campaign and remove my access at any time. Nobody needs to share a Facebook password.

Tips to avoid payment problems

  • Keep enough balance on a prepaid card. Failed payments can pause your ads and sometimes restrict the account.
  • Set a spending limit on the ad account in Billing so a campaign never spends more than you planned.
  • Save Meta's receipts every month for your accounts.
  • Do not keep adding and removing different cards. Frequent payment changes can trigger Meta's security checks.
