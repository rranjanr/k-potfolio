Facebook boost vs Meta Ads Manager: what is the difference?

The Boost button on a Facebook or Instagram post is the quick option. You pick a budget and a rough audience and the post gets shown to more people. It works for simple awareness, but you get limited control over placements, objectives and testing.

Meta Ads Manager is the full tool. It lets you choose a campaign objective (such as messages or leads), run more than one ad against each other, control exactly where the ad shows, exclude people who already follow you, and see a proper breakdown of results.

For most Nepali businesses I use both: boosts for quick posts like festival offers, and Ads Manager campaigns for anything where you want measurable messages or leads.

Ads built for how Nepal buys

Most customers in Nepal do not buy straight from an ad. They send a message first to ask the price, check stock or ask about delivery. That is why I usually set campaigns up to bring messages on Messenger, Instagram or WhatsApp, and why I treat replying to those messages as part of the ad work, not an extra.

For my current client, Rouniyar International, I run Meta ads on their product and wholesale content and reply to the leads those ads bring in. Being the same person who made the post and answers the inbox means I can see which ads bring serious buyers and which only bring likes.

Paying for ads from Nepal

Meta bills ads in foreign currency, so Nepali businesses usually pay with a card that supports US dollar payments (many Nepali banks issue dollar or dual-currency cards), or through a local agency that pays Meta and bills you in rupees. I can help you set up the payment method on your own ad account, so the account and its history stay yours.

FAQ

Meta ads and boosting questions

What is the difference between boosting and Meta Ads Manager?

Boosting promotes a single post with basic targeting. Meta Ads Manager gives full control over the campaign objective, audience, placements and testing. I use boosts for quick promotions and Ads Manager for campaigns where you want measurable messages or leads.

How much should I spend on Facebook ads in Nepal?

Start with a budget you are comfortable testing for two to four weeks, then increase spend on the ads that bring results. I suggest a starting budget based on your product, location and goal.

How do I pay for Facebook ads from Nepal?

Meta charges in foreign currency, so most businesses use a card that supports US dollar payments or pay through a local agency in rupees. I can help you set up payment on your own ad account.

Can you target customers in only one city?

Yes. Ads can target a city, a radius around your shop, or a list of towns, for example Biratnagar and Itahari, or specific parts of Kathmandu Valley.

Will you reply to the leads from ads?

Yes. Replying to leads is part of how I run ads, because most Nepali customers send a message about price and stock before they buy.

Do I need to give you my password?

No. I work through partner access in Meta Business Suite, and you can remove it at any time.
